    Severity scoring¶

    Every item gets a severity from 0 to 100 when it is stored, together with the list of reasons behind it. You can see the breakdown by clicking the score on the item page. This page lists every rule; the code is in app/processing/severity.py.

    Severity is a triage aid. It answers the question "how urgently should an analyst read this?" It is not a CVSS score and not a probability. Scam reports have their own fraud score, and the two are never added together.

    How the score is built¶

    severity = text signals            (4 groups: the strongest term, plus half the runner-up, in each group)
         + entity signals          (family, actor, CVEs, indicators, techniques)
         + structured-intel base   (pulses, analyses, scans)
         × dampeners               (patch note ×0.7, round-up / non-incident ×0.5)
         → cap for routine leak-site posts (35)
         → rounded, kept between 0 and 100

    After that, two things can still raise a stored score: an indicator that enrichment confirms as malicious, and the AI assessment. See After ingest.

    Text signals¶

    The title, the first 20,000 characters of the text, and the tags are matched against four signal groups. The terms within one group are different ways of saying the same kind of thing, so they must not all add up:

    • Only the strongest term in a group counts in full. The runner-up counts at half weight, and anything after that is ignored.
    • A term that appears in the title counts 1.5 times, because headlines name the real subject while the body mentions everything in passing. Not in the title of a roundup, a guide or other non-incident content: such a title lists topics, it isn't about them.
    • An English term and its Indonesian equivalent share a label and count once.
    • The contributions of the different groups are added together.

    Exploitation¶

    Signal Points Examples of what matches
    zero-day 30 zero-day, 0-day
    exploited in the wild 30 actively exploited, in the wild, under active attack, mass exploitation, sedang / aktif dieksploitasi
    listed in CISA KEV 28 KEV, known exploited vulnerability
    public exploit code 18 PoC released, exploit code published
    exploitation mentioned 10 exploit, exploited, exploiting, eksploitasi

    When the strongest exploitation term is worth 18 points or more (including the title bonus), the item counts as actively exploited. The patch-note dampener below then no longer applies, because a bug that is being exploited is still urgent even when a patch is available.

    Impact¶

    Signal Points Examples of what matches
    remote code execution 14 RCE, unauthenticated attacker, pre-auth, eksekusi kode jarak jauh
    destructive / wiper 14 wiper, destructive malware, data-destroying
    critical / CVSS ≥ 9 12 CVSS 9.8, "critical flaw", "rated critical", celah kritis (but not "critical infrastructure")
    authentication bypass 12 auth bypass, bypasses authentication
    service disruption 10 halts production or operations, shuts down plants or systems, disrupts operations, systems went offline, layanan lumpuh, sistem lumpuh, data terenkripsi
    privilege escalation 6 privilege escalation, LPE, EoP
    high severity 6 high-severity

    Entity signals¶

    The entities the extractor found are evidence in their own right. Each kind counts once:

    Signal Points
    A known malware family (from the knowledge base, a feed or a provider) 10
    Only an unknown family, found by the "Name ransomware" pattern 6
    A known threat actor 8
    CVEs 5 for the first, +2 for each additional CVE, up to 11
    Indicators (IPs, domains, URLs, hashes) 8, or 12 with five or more
    ATT&CK technique ids 3
    The item is a pulse or analysis / a scan (structured intel) 6 / 4

    Scam types, brands, scammer identifiers and sectors add no severity points (see the order of the ingest steps).

    Dampeners and caps¶

    These are applied after everything above has been added up, in this order. Apart from the leak-site cap, they only look at the title:

    Rule Effect Triggered by
    Patch or advisory × 0.7 "fixes", "patches", "Patch Tuesday", "security update", "addresses", "hotfix", tambal, pembaruan keamanan… — unless the item is actively exploited (see above)
    Round-up, guide or non-incident × 0.5 "week in review", "roundup", "top 10", "how to", "what is", webinar, podcast, survey, "announces", funding, acquisition, training, tips; outages, pricing, lawsuits, GDPR; cara mengatasi, begini cara, kenali, apa itu, seminar, peluncuran, promo, diskon
    Routine leak-site posting capped at 35 A post saying a group "added / claims / listed … victims / leak site". Ransomware trackers publish hundreds of these every day

    Both multipliers can apply to the same item (× 0.35 in total). The result is rounded and kept between 0 and 100. Each reduction appears in the breakdown as a reason with negative points, so the reasons always add up to the score (allowing for rounding).

    Worked examples¶

    All three were calculated by running the text through score_item.

    1. An exploited zero-day. "LockBit ransomware exploits Citrix zero-day to hit Indonesian hospitals", with a body describing an actively exploited RCE, one CVE, one technique and three defanged indicators:

    Reason Points How
    zero-day 45 30, in the title × 1.5; the strongest exploitation term
    exploited in the wild 15 30 × 0.5 as the runner-up in exploitation
    remote code execution 14 The strongest impact term
    ransomware 18 12, in the title × 1.5; the strongest threat class term
    active campaign 10 The strongest activity term
    sensitive sector 4 5 × 1.5 (hospitals, in the title) × 0.5 as the runner-up
    known malware family 10 LockBit
    1 CVE(s) 5
    3 indicator(s) 8
    ATT&CK techniques 3
    Total 132 → 100 Capped

    2. A patch note. "Microsoft fixes critical Exchange flaw in Patch Tuesday update", with a body mentioning one CVE and remote code execution:

    Reason Points How
    critical / CVSS ≥ 9 18 12, in the title × 1.5; the strongest impact term
    remote code execution 7 14 × 0.5 as the runner-up in impact
    1 CVE(s) 5
    patch / advisory only −9 (18 + 7 + 5) × 0.7 = 21. There is no exploitation signal, so the dampener applies
    Total 21

    If the body had also said "exploited in the wild", the item would have scored 65 with no dampener: exploited in the wild 30, exploitation mentioned 5 (10 × 0.5 as the runner-up, from the word "exploited"), and then 18 + 7 + 5 as before.

    3. A scam warning. "Waspada penipuan APK undangan pernikahan mengatasnamakan BRI, korban rugi Rp 1,2 miliar" scores a severity of only 9 (penipuan in the title: phishing / social engineering, 6 × 1.5). The same item has a fraud score of 83: malicious APK lure 28, also impersonation 4, impersonates BRI 10, losses of Rp 1.2 billion 15, 120 victims 10, scammer identifiers 8, and a police warning 8. That is intended. As a cyber threat it is minor, but as a scam report it is serious, and it ranks high on the scam & fraud dashboard.

    After ingest¶

    Event Effect on the stored score Reason shown
    One of the item's indicators turns malicious in enrichment +12 indicator confirmed malicious (ip)
    One of the item's indicators turns suspicious +5. If it later turns malicious, the score rises by another 7 and the reason shows +12 indicator confirmed suspicious (…)
    A malicious or suspicious indicator is re-checked and its verdict goes down The item keeps the bonus of its strongest remaining indicator, or loses it Updated, or removed
    An analyst sets an indicator's verdict The bonus follows the analyst's verdict, up or down Updated, or removed
    An analyst removes an entity from the item (wrong entity here, wrong entity everywhere, not a threat) The item is re-scored from the links that remain, keeping the enrichment bonus and the AI assessment The entity's reason disappears
    The AI rates the item higher than the rule-based score The score is raised towards the AI's number, by at most +20. It never goes below the rule-based score AI assessment (capped), with an AI badge

    Each item keeps only one indicator confirmed reason, for its strongest verdict, so several malicious indicators don't add +12 each. The AI assessment is replaced rather than added to when an item is analysed again: its old points are taken off first, so a lower new assessment can remove them. Both adjustments are capped at 100.

    Recomputing scores¶

    When the terms, weights or dampeners change in a new version, the rules version (severity.RULES_VERSION) goes up, and every stored item is re-scored once, about 90 seconds after the new version first starts (jobs.run_rescore_if_outdated). That way old and new items are scored by the same rules before the trends compare them. You can also re-score by hand:

    Tool What it starts from Keeps the enrichment bonus Keeps the AI assessment
    Re-score all items (Settings), manage.py rescore The stored text and the entities already linked (with how they were found). Nothing is re-extracted and no network calls are made Yes Yes
    manage.py reextract A fresh extraction of the stored text, which rebuilds the regex, kb and heuristic links (except those analysts removed) and the scam and sector analysis Yes Yes

    In every case, the points that don't come from the text are added back afterwards (severity.carry_over): the indicator confirmed bonus for the strongest verdict among the item's indicators today, and the last AI assessment. The AI assessment remembers the severity the model proposed, so it is applied again to the new score with the same cap. If the new score already reaches the model's number, it adds nothing. AI assessments stored before version 0.11 don't have a proposed severity, so they keep their original points.

    Slimmed items (past the full-text retention period) are re-scored from their snippet, so their score may drop. See Housekeeping and re-analysis.

    Reading a score¶

    Range Typical content
    80–100 Exploited zero-days, supply-chain compromises, ransomware campaigns with indicators
    50–79 Active campaigns, new malware with indicators, critical vulnerabilities, significant breaches
    20–49 Breach and malware news without technical detail, structured intel without strong text signals, patched critical bugs
    0–19 Round-ups, guides, product news, scam warnings (check their fraud score), routine leak-site postings

    Webhooks use 70 as the default threshold for a "high severity" item, and AI auto-summaries only run from 40 upwards.
