    Trends and correlation¶

    This page explains how the dashboards turn stored items into trend scores, CVE priorities, breakdowns and lists of related items. None of these figures are stored: they are calculated from the items whenever a page is opened (trend lists are cached for 45 seconds). The code is in app/processing/trends.py, app/processing/graph.py and app/intel/cve.py.

    Counting rules¶

    Three rules decide what a number on the dashboards actually counts.

    Stories, not copies. When several outlets publish the same article, the original and its copies count as one story (see Cross-source duplicates). This rule applies everywhere JATMP counts how much was reported:

    • the KPI tiles and their sparklines, and the timelines;
    • the breakdown charts (malware and actor types, sectors, scam types, brands);
    • target countries and provinces;
    • mention counts in the entity lists;
    • trend scores and the scam spreading check;
    • the AI executive summary and the PDF / Word reports.

    The Stories collected tile also shows the number of items, copies included, when the two differ.

    Per-outlet figures count items. Items per source, source countries and languages describe what each outlet published, so there a copy counts for the outlet that carried it.

    Dates and regions. Periods are measured by publication date, so backfilled history lands in the period it was published in, not the day it was collected. In the National (ID) view, every figure only includes items that target Indonesia, or that come from an Indonesian outlet without naming another target (see The National view).

    Periods and baselines¶

    Period Chart bars Trend baseline
    7, 15 or 30 days One per day The 30 days before the period
    3 months (90 days) One per day The 90 days before the period
    6 months, 1 year One per week (the first day of each 7-day block) As long as the period itself

    In other words, the baseline is always the max(30, period) days immediately before the period. The live collectors only cover the recent past, so the longer periods (and their baselines) only become meaningful after a backfill. The dashboards tell you when some sources have gaps.

    Trend score¶

    Malware families, threat actors and CVEs on the threat radar, in the Trending tables and in the AI summary are ranked by a trend score. For one entity, with a period of W days and a baseline of B days, JATMP uses:

    Input Meaning
    r Stories that mention it in the period
    b Stories that mention it in the baseline
    s How many different sources reported it in the period (originals only; at least 1)
    c How many different source types (news, social, intel) reported it in the period (at least 1) 
    growth = (r / W + 0.1) / (b / B + 0.1)
score  = r × √min(growth, 10) × (1 + 0.25 × (s − 1)) × (1 + 0.2 × (c − 1))

    Each part has a job:

    • Volume (r) is the starting point. Something mentioned only once doesn't trend, however new it is.
    • Growth compares the daily rate now with the daily rate before. The + 0.1 stops a first mention from producing infinite growth, and the square root and the cap at 10 stop a sudden spike from drowning out threats that are steady but widely covered.
    • Spread rewards independent confirmation: each extra source adds 25%, and each extra source type adds 20%. Syndicated copies don't count as extra sources.
    Example (7-day period, 30-day baseline) r b s c growth score
    A family that suddenly shows up in the news and on Bluesky 6 3 4 2 4.79 27.56
    A family that is always in the news at this rate 6 26 4 2 0.99 12.54
    A single blog that mentions a new name twice 2 0 1 1 3.86 3.93
    An outbreak that everyone covers 20 0 8 3 29.57 (capped at 10) 243.5

    A few more rules:

    • An entity with no story in the period isn't listed at all.
    • A malware name that was only ever found by the "Name ransomware" pattern only appears once two sources name it in the period, so a single misread headline can't make something trend.
    • An entity first seen during the period gets the new badge. To list only such entities, use the ✦ New threats button on the Intelligence lists (Malware, Threat actors, CVEs, IOCs, Scam types, Impersonated brands), which keeps the entries whose first-seen date falls in the chosen period; see Using JATMP.
    • Actors are grouped by type (nation-state, ransomware, extortion, cybercrime or unclassified), using the knowledge base or a category you set in data/knowledge_extra.json.

    Trend lists are cached in memory for 45 seconds for each combination of type, period, region and list length. The cache is cleared whenever a collection run stores new items.

    CVE priority¶

    Lots of chatter about a vulnerability doesn't necessarily mean it should be patched first. So trending CVEs are re-ranked by how likely they are to be exploited:

    priority = trend score × (2 if in CISA KEV) × (1 + EPSS) × (0.5 + CVSS / 10)

    A CVE without a CVSS score is multiplied by 1 for that part. The dashboard takes the 60 CVEs with the highest trend scores, sorts them by priority, shows the top 8, and lists the families and actors mentioned alongside each one.

    For example, take two CVEs that both have a trend score of 10. The first is in KEV, with an EPSS of 0.6 and a CVSS of 9.8: 10 × 2 × 1.6 × 1.48 = 47.4. The second isn't in KEV, with an EPSS of 0.01 and a CVSS of 5.3: 10 × 1 × 1.01 × 1.03 = 10.4.

    CVEs by exploitation risk¶

    This chart puts each CVE mentioned in the period into the first bucket it qualifies for:

    Bucket Condition

    Correlation¶

    The Correlated items card on an item's page (and in its preview) ranks other items by how strong the evidence they share is, not just by how many entities they have in common.

    Each shared entity contributes:

    weight(type) / log2(2 + mention_count)

    so a rare entity counts for more than one that is mentioned everywhere.

    Type Weight Type Weight
    sha256, sha1, md5 6.0 crypto wallet 6.0
    url 5.0 bank account, e-wallet 5.0
    domain 4.0 phone 4.0
    ip 3.0 brand 1.0
    malware, actor 2.5 scam type 0.5
    cve 1.5 sector 0.2
    technique 0.5

    An item's score is the sum over all the entities it shares. It is then multiplied by 1.3 if the item comes from a different source type (a news article and a Bluesky post, say), or by 1.15 if it comes from a different source of the same type. Matches through an indicator are flagged as strong. The top 15 are shown.

    Newsletters and round-ups. A digest (Cisco Talos' Threat Source, weekly round-ups, podcasts) covers several unrelated stories and repeats a "most prevalent files" list every issue, so what it shares with another item is weak evidence. JATMP treats an item as a digest when:

    • its source's config has "digest": true (false opts a source out, and then nothing else is checked). The source form in Settings has no field for it yet; it is kept in the source's stored config, and saving the form leaves it in place;
    • its title or tags use round-up wording (newsletter, threat source, week in review, roundup, bulletin, in other news, weekly threat report, daily briefing, mingguan, kilas siber…). Words that the scam and sector analysis treat as round-ups but that name a single story here (interview, as in the Contagious Interview campaign, lessons from, webinar) don't count;
    • or its body carries newsletter boilerplate (Welcome to this week's edition…, Upcoming events where you can find, most prevalent malware files). Talos issue titles such as "Never be afraid to ask questions" say nothing, so the title alone is not enough.

    When one of the two items is a digest, the score is multiplied by 0.4, and by 0.1 when both are. Two issues of the same source are never linked (what they share is the recurring boilerplate), and a digest link whose discounted score falls below 1 is dropped before the source bonus above is applied. The links that remain carry a digest badge and are never flagged strong.

    The flag is stored on the item (items.is_digest) when it is collected. After an upgrade that changes the detection rules, run_digesttag sets it again on every stored item, and manage.py digesttag does the same by hand (for example after changing a source's "digest" setting). An item whose text retention has already trimmed keeps a digest flag it had, since the body phrases that proved it are gone.

    Co-mentions. Mentioned together with on an entity's page and in its preview, the indicators listed on a family's page, the related chips on the lists and the related list of GET /api/v1/entities/{id} count the entities that appear in the same items, and they leave out digests entirely: a hash from a newsletter's weekly most prevalent files list sits next to the family of another story, and that is no evidence the two belong together. Even outside digests, being in the same article is not attribution (on this deployment's data, an indicator and a family from the same article agreed with the provider's family only about a third of the time). So on an indicator's page the card is called Seen in the same items as and says so, and Linked by enrichment (what a provider attributes) is shown above it.

    To keep this fast, entities mentioned in more than 400 items aren't used to find candidates (although they still add weight to candidates found through rarer entities), and at most the 4,000 most recent links are read.

    Relationship graph¶

    The graph on an item's page is built from:

    1. the item itself, in the centre;
    2. its entities (indicators with a bad verdict first), taking up at most 45% of the nodes;
    3. whatever enrichment linked them to (a hash's family, a domain's IPs, a family's OTX indicators), at most 25% of the nodes;
    4. the top related items from the correlation above, with lines to the entities they share.

    On an entity's page, the graph shows the entity, its enrichment links, the families, actors and CVEs most often mentioned with it, and the 10 highest-severity items that mention it. The graph starts with up to 60 nodes; Show more raises the limit to 150. When something had to be left out, the graph says so.

    Reading the lines. Under the graph, a second legend row shows each kind of line that appears in it, what it means and whose claim it is. Hover an entry for the explanation; hovering a line in the graph shows its label and the same claim.

    Line Means Claim
    Solid, grey (Mentions) The item names the entity: extracted from its text by the platform's rules, or listed by the source's own indicator feed automatic
    Dashed, green (Provider link) A threat-intel provider (OTX, urlscan, MalwareBazaar…) states the relation, such as an indicator attributed to a family provider's claim
    Solid, blue (Also mentions) Another item names the same entity: not proof that the two items describe the same activity needs validation
    Dotted (Mentioned together) Two entities named in the same items: not attribution needs validation

    Needs validation marks relations that come from co-occurrence only. An analyst can remove a wrong link with Correct (Analyst corrections). Newsletters and round-ups are left out of Mentioned together; an Also mentions line can still lead to one, because those lines come from the correlation above, where a digest counts for less.

    Other figures¶

    Widget What it shows
    KPI tiles Stories in the period compared with the period before; families and actors first seen in the period; indicators seen in the period, and how many of them are malicious
    Sparklines Stories, plus the number of different families, indicators, CVEs and actors mentioned, per day (per week for periods over 90 days)
    Timeline Stories per day (or week), split by source type
    Threat actors by type Stories that mention an actor, per type of actor
    Critical infrastructure sectors Stories per sector, with all eight sectors listed
    Items per source Items per source, copies included
    Indicators by type and verdict Indicators last seen in the period, split into malicious, suspicious and the rest
    Geography and the globe Target countries of the stories in the period (top 10 on the globe); source countries and languages count items
    Scam & fraud dashboard The same rules, applied to scam reports (items with a fraud score above 0). Spreading means at least 5 reports this week and twice as many as the week before
