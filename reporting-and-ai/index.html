    Reporting and AI¶

    This page covers the Threat Intel Findings report builder and the optional AI features. How the AI features work in detail, and what they send to a model, is explained in AI processing.

    Reporting¶

    The report builder produces a Threat Intel Findings document for any period, as PDF, Word, Markdown or CSV.

    The report opens with a summary of the period and then writes up the top findings: when each was published, its severity, a description, the related malware, actors and CVEs, and a link to the original. It then lists the leading malware families, with their type, the sources that covered them and an example hash, and closes with the top indicators of compromise and their verdicts.

    You can also add collection statistics, actors, CVEs, newly seen families and the latest AI executive summary, and you can choose how many findings, families and indicators to include. Like the dashboards, the report counts a story carried by several outlets once.

    AI (optional)¶

    With an AI model configured, JATMP can:

    • summarise and analyse individual items;
    • write a threat brief about a family, actor or other entity;
    • write a daily executive summary;
    • answer questions about the collected data.

    Setting it up¶

    Setting up the AI is a three-way choice between Off, Local and Cloud. You then pick a vendor, and a model from the live list that the server or provider reports:

    • Local runtimes: Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM, Jan, or any OpenAI-compatible server.
    • Cloud providers: Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Groq, Gemini, Mistral, DeepSeek, xAI, Together, or any OpenAI-compatible API.

    You can also choose the language the AI writes in. The model names in the presets were current when this version was released; use Load models to see what your server or provider actually offers.

    Personal data¶

    Item text can contain phone numbers, bank accounts and e-mail addresses. With a cloud model, JATMP replaces them with placeholders ([phone 1], [account 1] and so on) before a prompt is sent, so they never reach the provider. The setting Personal data in prompts (Settings → AI) controls this: cloud (the default) redacts for cloud models only, always redacts for every model, and never switches it off. See What leaves your network.

    The executive summary¶

    The executive summary appears on the dashboard card and behind the AI summary button, and older ones are kept under AI insights. It is short on purpose: a one-sentence headline, an overall threat level (low, elevated, high or critical), three or four key points, and up to three actions for today. It is based on the last 24 hours of items plus the week's trending malware, actors, CVEs, scam types and sectors.

    The model answers in JSON. JATMP checks the answer, removes any Markdown or maths notation, and displays it with the same components as the rest of the interface. If the answer can't be used, the text is shown as it came.

    The summary runs every day at 06:00 UTC by default. You can change the time and the time zone under Settings → General; see Daily schedule.

    Asking questions¶

    Ask on the AI insights page answers a question from the data JATMP has collected. Before the model sees the question, JATMP picks the items that fit it best: the families, actors, CVEs and indicators the question names (aliases included, so "Fancy Bear" finds APT28), the malware types and sectors it mentions ("ransomware on hospitals" finds "LockBit hits a healthcare provider"), the period it names ("this week", "minggu ini"), and its words through the full-text index. A syndicated story counts once.

    The answer cites the items it is based on as [1], [2] and so on, each linking to the item, and lists below it which items were used and why they matched. The model is told to answer only from those items and to say when something is missing. See Questions.
